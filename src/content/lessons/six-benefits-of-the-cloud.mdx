AWS describes six advantages of cloud computing. The exam expects you to
recognize each one from a short scenario, so learn what each means and the kind
of situation it answers. They all trace back to one idea from the last lesson:
you pay for what you use instead of buying hardware up front.

<Callout type="key" title="The six, in order">
Trade fixed expense for variable expense. Benefit from massive economies of
scale. Stop guessing capacity. Increase speed and agility. Stop spending money
running and maintaining data centers. Go global in minutes.
</Callout>

## Trade fixed expense for variable expense

A fixed expense is money you commit before you know how much you will use, such
as buying a rack of servers. A variable expense tracks actual usage. In the
cloud you pay only when you consume resources and only for how much you consume.
A team that buys ten servers for a launch pays for all ten whether or not the
traffic arrives. The same team on AWS runs what the traffic needs and stops
paying when it stops.

## Benefit from massive economies of scale

A provider that serves hundreds of thousands of customers buys hardware, power,
and networking at a scale no single company can match. That aggregated demand
lowers the provider's cost, and AWS passes the lower cost through as lower
pay-as-you-go prices. You get a price you could not reach buying for yourself.

## Stop guessing capacity

When you size infrastructure before you deploy, you usually guess wrong. Guess
high and you pay for idle machines. Guess low and you run out at the worst
moment. The cloud removes the guess. You request as much or as little capacity
as you need and scale up or down within minutes as demand changes.

<Callout type="note" title="Guessing versus scaling">
A retailer cannot know exact traffic on a sale day. On AWS it does not have to.
It scales out when orders surge and scales back in when they fall, paying for
the peak only while the peak lasts.
</Callout>

## Increase speed and agility

When new resources are a few clicks away, the time to get them drops from weeks
to minutes. That speed lowers the cost of trying an idea, so teams experiment
more and ship faster. The advantage is not only the hardware; it is the agility
that comes from making the cost and delay of experimenting small.

## Stop spending money running and maintaining data centers

Running a data center means racking servers, powering and cooling them, and
replacing failed parts. None of that work makes your product better for a
customer. Moving to the cloud lets a business hand the heavy lifting to AWS and
put its effort into the projects that set it apart.

## Go global in minutes

AWS operates in many locations worldwide, so you can deploy your application
across several of them with a few clicks. Serving users from a location near
them lowers latency and gives a better experience, at low cost. A service like
Amazon CloudFront extends this further by caching content close to users around
the world.

## What to take away

Match each benefit to the scenario it answers: variable expense for upfront cost
worries, economies of scale for pricing, stop guessing for capacity planning,
speed and agility for slow provisioning, stop running data centers for
operational burden, and go global in minutes for reaching distant users. The
wording in a question points you to the advantage it is testing.
