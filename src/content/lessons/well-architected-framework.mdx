The AWS Well-Architected Framework is a set of guidance for designing and
running systems in the cloud. It organizes design principles and best practices
into pillars, each covering one area of concern, so you can evaluate an
architecture and find where it falls short. Reviewing an architecture this way
is a constructive conversation about the decisions behind it, not an audit
mechanism: a review produces areas to improve, not a pass-or-fail grade. The
exam expects you to name the pillars and recognize which one a scenario is
about.

<Callout type="key" title="The six pillars">
Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost
Optimization, and Sustainability. There are six. Sustainability is the one
people forget, so memorize the full set.
</Callout>

## Operational Excellence

Operational Excellence is about running and monitoring systems to deliver
business value and improving the supporting processes and procedures over time.
It covers how you organize your team, make changes safely, and learn from how the
workload behaves in production so operations get better with each cycle.

## Security

The Security pillar is the ability to protect data, systems, and assets and to
use cloud technologies to improve your security. It includes controlling who can
do what, protecting data in transit and at rest, and detecting and responding to
events. This is the design counterpart to the security topics in domain 2.

## Reliability

The Reliability pillar is the ability of a workload to perform its intended
function correctly and consistently when expected, and to recover from failures.
A reliable design plans for things to break and keeps working anyway, for
example by spreading across Availability Zones and recovering automatically.

AWS lists five design principles for reliability.

- Automatically recover from failure: monitor key indicators and run automation
  when a threshold is breached, so failures are repaired without waiting for a
  person.
- Test recovery procedures: simulate failures in the cloud and validate the
  recovery path, exposing problems before a real event.
- Scale horizontally to increase aggregate workload availability: replace one
  large resource with several small ones and spread requests across them so
  they do not share a common point of failure.
- Stop guessing capacity: monitor demand and add or remove resources
  automatically instead of over-provisioning or under-provisioning.
- Manage change through automation: change infrastructure through automation so
  every change can be tracked and reviewed.

<Callout type="note" title="Reliability versus Performance Efficiency">
Reliability asks whether the workload keeps doing its job when something fails.
Performance Efficiency asks whether it uses resources well to meet its
performance needs. A scenario about staying up points to Reliability; one about
using the right resources efficiently points to Performance Efficiency.
</Callout>

## Performance Efficiency

The Performance Efficiency pillar is the ability to use cloud resources
efficiently to meet performance requirements and to keep that efficiency as
demand changes and technologies evolve. It is about choosing the right resource
types and sizes for the workload and revisiting those choices as better options
become available.

## Cost Optimization

The Cost Optimization pillar is the ability to run systems that deliver business
value at the lowest price point. It covers paying only for what you need,
choosing the right pricing models, and stopping spend on resources that are idle
or oversized.

AWS lists five design principles for cost optimization.

- Implement cloud financial management: build cost management as a capability,
  with the people and processes to match.
- Adopt a consumption model: pay only for the resources you consume, for example
  by stopping development environments outside work hours.
- Measure overall efficiency: weigh the business output of a workload against
  the cost of delivering it.
- Stop spending money on undifferentiated heavy lifting: let AWS run the data
  centers and managed services so your people work on customers and products.
- Analyze and attribute expenditure: identify what each workload costs and
  attribute it to revenue streams and individual workload owners.

## Sustainability

The Sustainability pillar focuses on reducing the environmental impact of running
cloud workloads, especially energy consumption and efficiency. It encourages
choices that get more useful work from the resources you provision, such as
right-sizing and using managed services that run efficiently at scale.

## General design principles

Beyond the pillars, the framework lists six general design principles.

- Stop guessing your capacity needs: use as much or as little capacity as you
  need and scale automatically.
- Test systems at production scale: create a production-scale test environment
  on demand and shut it down when the testing is over.
- Automate with architectural experimentation in mind: automation makes
  workloads cheap to create and replicate, and changes easy to track and undo.
- Consider evolutionary architectures: let a design keep changing instead of
  freezing it at launch.
- Drive architectures using data: collect data on how your choices affect the
  workload and decide from that.
- Improve through game days: regularly schedule simulations of events in
  production to find weak spots and build the team's experience of them.

## The AWS Well-Architected Tool

The AWS Well-Architected Tool is a service you open in the AWS Management
Console with any AWS account. You define a workload, answer the framework's
questions across each pillar, and the tool records those decisions and returns
an improvement plan: a prioritized list of issues, with recommendations for
making the workload more reliable, secure, efficient, and cost-effective. AWS
offers it at no charge, and you pay only for the underlying AWS resources you
use. It measures and guides: the output is a plan to improve, not a compliance
result, and the design work still belongs to you.

## What to take away

Be able to list all six pillars and tell them apart from a short scenario.
Operational Excellence is about running and improving operations, Security about
protecting data and systems, Reliability about working and recovering through
failures, Performance Efficiency about using resources well, Cost Optimization
about the lowest price point, and Sustainability about reducing environmental
impact. Know the six general design principles by name, and know that the
Well-Architected Tool is the free console review.
