AWS pricing rewards two behaviors: paying only for what you use, and committing
to usage you can predict. The exam tests whether you can pick the right purchase
option for a workload and recognize the few cases where a bill grows in ways
beginners do not expect. This lesson covers the pricing principles, the EC2
purchase options, the Free Tier as it works now, and the basics of data transfer
pricing.

## The pricing principles

Three ideas sit under almost every AWS price.

- Pay as you go. You pay for the resources you consume while you consume them,
  with no large upfront purchase and no charge for capacity you are not using.
- Save when you commit. When you can predict steady usage, you commit to it for a
  one-year or three-year term and pay less per unit than On-Demand. Savings Plans
  are the main way to do this.
- Pay less by using more. For services such as Amazon S3 and data transfer out,
  pricing is tiered, so the price per unit drops as your usage climbs into higher
  tiers.

<Callout type="key" title="Two levers, lower cost">
Usage-based billing lowers cost by letting you stop paying for idle resources.
Commitment lowers cost by trading flexibility for a discount. A scenario about
steady, predictable load points to a commitment option; a scenario about
unpredictable or temporary load points to On-Demand.
</Callout>

## EC2 purchase options

Amazon EC2 has four purchase options you should be able to tell apart.

- On-Demand. You pay by the second for the instances you launch, with no
  commitment. Use it for short-term, spiky, or unpredictable workloads, and for
  anything you are still testing.
- Savings Plans. You commit to a consistent amount of usage measured in dollars
  per hour for a one-year or three-year term, in exchange for a lower rate than
  On-Demand. Use it when you can commit to a spend level but want flexibility
  across instance choices.
- Reserved Instances. You commit to a consistent instance configuration, such as
  instance type and Region, for a one-year or three-year term, in exchange for a
  lower rate. Use it for steady-state workloads with a known configuration.
- Spot Instances. You request spare EC2 capacity at a steep discount, and AWS can
  reclaim it when it needs the capacity back. Use it for fault-tolerant or
  flexible work that can be interrupted, such as batch jobs and testing.

<Callout type="tip" title="Pick the option from the workload">
Interruptible and flexible on timing is Spot. Steady and predictable, and you
want the lowest committed rate, is a Savings Plan or a Reserved Instance.
Anything short-lived, new, or unpredictable is On-Demand.
</Callout>

## The AWS Free Tier

AWS recently restructured how new accounts start, so verify this rather than
trusting older summaries. A new account begins on a free plan that includes up to
200 US dollars in AWS credits: 100 US dollars when you sign up, and up to 100
more as you use a set of foundational services. The free plan lasts up to six
months, or until you use up the credits, whichever comes first. When you are
ready for production, you move to the paid plan, where pay-as-you-go pricing
applies.

Two other free categories matter. Always free offers cover more than 30 services
that stay free up to a monthly usage limit for as long as you are a customer, on
both the free and paid plans. Short-term trials let you try selected paid
services free for a limited period or up to a one-time limit. Customers who were
already on the older Free Tier keep its benefits unchanged.

<Callout type="note" title="The Free Tier changed">
The current new-account experience centers on credits and a six-month free plan,
plus always free offers and short-term trials. The older twelve-month framing is
out of date for new sign-ups, so answer Free Tier questions from the current
structure.
</Callout>

## Data transfer pricing

The pattern to remember: data coming in is usually free, and data going out is
usually charged. Moving data into AWS from the internet generally costs nothing.
Sending data out to the internet is billed, though AWS gives every customer a
free allowance of 100 GB of data transfer out to the internet each month,
aggregated across services and Regions, with some exceptions. Beyond that
allowance, outbound transfer is priced in tiers, so heavy senders pay less per
gigabyte at higher volumes.

This is the cost beginners overlook, because the resource itself, such as the
storage holding the data, looks cheap while the egress to move it out adds up.

## What to take away

AWS pricing comes down to pay as you go, save when you commit, and pay less by
using more. The four EC2 purchase options are On-Demand for flexibility, Savings
Plans and Reserved Instances for committed discounts, and Spot for interruptible
work at the lowest price. The Free Tier now gives new accounts credits and a
six-month free plan alongside always free offers and short-term trials. And
remember that data transfer in is generally free while data transfer out is
charged after the monthly free allowance.
