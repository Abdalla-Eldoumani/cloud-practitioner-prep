AWS pricing rewards two behaviors: paying only for what you use, and committing
to usage you can predict. The exam tests whether you can pick the right purchase
option for a workload and recognize the few cases where a bill grows in ways
beginners do not expect. This lesson covers the pricing principles, the EC2
purchase options, the Free Tier as it works now, and the basics of data transfer
pricing.

## The pricing principles

Three ideas sit under almost every AWS price.

- Pay as you go. You pay for the resources you consume while you consume them,
  with no large upfront purchase and no charge for capacity you are not using.
- Save when you commit. When you can predict steady usage, you commit to it for a
  one-year or three-year term and pay less per unit than On-Demand. Savings Plans
  are the main way to do this.
- Pay less by using more. For services such as Amazon S3 and data transfer out,
  pricing is tiered, so the price per unit drops as your usage climbs into higher
  tiers.

<Callout type="key" title="Two levers, lower cost">
Usage-based billing lowers cost by letting you stop paying for idle resources.
Commitment lowers cost by trading flexibility for a discount. A scenario about
steady, predictable load points to a commitment option; a scenario about
unpredictable or temporary load points to On-Demand.
</Callout>

## EC2 purchase options

The exam guide names seven ways to buy EC2 capacity. Start with these four,
which carry almost every scenario you will be asked about.

- On-Demand. You pay by the second for the instances you launch, with no
  commitment. Use it for short-term, irregular workloads that cannot be
  interrupted.
- Savings Plans. You commit to a consistent amount of usage measured in dollars
  per hour for a one-year or three-year term, in exchange for a lower rate than
  On-Demand. Use it when you can commit to a spend level but want flexibility
  across instance choices.
- Reserved Instances. You commit to a consistent instance configuration, such as
  instance type and Region, for a one-year or three-year term, in exchange for a
  lower rate. Use it for steady-state workloads with a known configuration.
- Spot Instances. You request spare EC2 capacity at a steep discount, and AWS can
  reclaim it when it needs the capacity back. Use it for fault-tolerant or
  flexible work that can be interrupted, such as batch jobs and testing. When AWS
  reclaims the capacity it sends a Spot Instance interruption notice two minutes
  before it stops or terminates the instance, and that window is all your
  application gets to checkpoint or drain its work.

### The two compute Savings Plans

A Compute Savings Plan is the flexible one. It applies to your EC2 usage
whatever the instance family, instance size, Region, operating system, or
tenancy, and it also covers AWS Fargate and AWS Lambda usage, for savings of up
to 66 percent off On-Demand rates. An EC2 Instance Savings Plan gives the deeper
discount, up to 72 percent, but you commit to one instance family in one Region.
Inside that family and Region it still follows changes in instance size,
operating system, and tenancy.

### Reserved Instance detail

A Reserved Instance is not a separate instance you launch. It is a billing
discount applied to matching On-Demand usage in your account, which is why the
running instance has to match attributes such as instance type and Region for
the discount to land. You also pick how to pay: All Upfront, Partial Upfront, or
No Upfront, and the larger the upfront payment, the more you generally save.

The offering class is the other choice. Standard Reserved Instances give the
larger discount but cannot be exchanged. Convertible Reserved Instances give a
smaller discount and can be exchanged during the term for another Convertible
Reserved Instance with different attributes, such as a different instance family
or tenancy.

### Dedicated hardware and reserved capacity

Three more options round out the list the exam guide names.

- Dedicated Instances run on hardware dedicated to a single AWS account, so they
  are physically isolated from instances belonging to other accounts. They bill
  per instance and give you no visibility into or control over instance
  placement.
- Dedicated Hosts give you a whole physical server dedicated to your use. They
  bill per host, show you the number of sockets and physical cores, and let you
  keep instances on the same host over time. That is what makes them the option
  for bringing your own per-socket, per-core, or per-VM licenses.
- Capacity Reservations reserve capacity for your instances in a specific
  Availability Zone for any duration. They buy capacity assurance, not a lower
  price: a Capacity Reservation carries no billing discount of its own, though
  you can combine one with a Savings Plan or a Regional Reserved Instance.

<Callout type="tip" title="Pick the option from the workload">
Interruptible and flexible on timing is Spot. Steady and predictable, and you
want the lowest committed rate, is a Savings Plan or a Reserved Instance.
Short-lived or unpredictable work that cannot be interrupted is On-Demand.
Dedicated hardware for licensing or isolation is a Dedicated Host or a
Dedicated Instance, and guaranteed capacity in one Availability Zone is a
Capacity Reservation.
</Callout>

## The AWS Free Tier

AWS recently restructured how new accounts start, so verify this rather than
trusting older summaries. At sign-up you choose between two plans, free and paid.
New customers get up to 200 US dollars in AWS credits on either one: 100 US
dollars when you sign up, and up to 100 more as you use a set of foundational
services. The free plan lasts up to six months, or until you use up the credits,
whichever comes first. When you are ready for production, you move to the paid
plan, where the full range of services is available at pay-as-you-go pricing.

Two other free categories matter. Always free offers cover more than 30 services
that stay free up to a monthly usage limit for as long as you are a customer, on
both the free and paid plans. Short-term trials let you try selected paid
services free for a limited period or up to a one-time limit. Customers who were
already on the older Free Tier keep its benefits unchanged.

<Callout type="note" title="The Free Tier changed">
The current new-account experience centers on credits and a six-month free plan,
plus always free offers and short-term trials. The older twelve-month framing is
out of date for new sign-ups, so answer Free Tier questions from the current
structure.
</Callout>

## Data transfer pricing

The pattern to remember: data coming in is usually free, and data going out is
usually charged. Moving data into AWS from the internet generally costs nothing.
Sending data out to the internet is billed, though AWS gives every customer a
free allowance of 100 GB of data transfer out to the internet each month,
aggregated across services and Regions, with some exceptions. Beyond that
allowance, outbound transfer is priced in tiers, so heavy senders pay less per
gigabyte at higher volumes.

This is the cost beginners overlook, because the resource itself, such as the
storage holding the data, looks cheap while the egress to move it out adds up.

## What to take away

AWS pricing comes down to pay as you go, save when you commit, and pay less by
using more. Among the EC2 purchase options, On-Demand buys flexibility, Savings
Plans and Reserved Instances buy committed discounts, Spot buys the lowest price
for interruptible work, and Dedicated Hosts, Dedicated Instances, and Capacity
Reservations cover dedicated hardware and capacity assurance. The Free Tier now
gives new accounts credits and a six-month free plan alongside always free
offers and short-term trials. And remember that data transfer in is generally
free while data transfer out is charged after the monthly free allowance.
