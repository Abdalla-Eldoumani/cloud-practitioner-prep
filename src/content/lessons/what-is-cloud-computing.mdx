Cloud computing is the on-demand delivery of IT resources over the internet with
pay-as-you-go pricing. That one sentence carries three ideas, and the exam leans
on all three.

On-demand means you start and stop resources when you need them, not months in
advance. Over the internet means you reach those resources from anywhere through
a console, a command line, or an API. Pay-as-you-go means you are billed for what
you consume rather than for hardware you bought and hoped to grow into.

<Callout type="key" title="Memorize the definition">
On-demand, over the internet, pay-as-you-go. If a question describes resources
you can turn on in minutes and pay for only while they run, it is describing
cloud computing.
</Callout>

## The client-server model

Almost everything in AWS follows the client-server model. A client, which is a
person or an application, sends a request. A server receives it, checks that the
caller is allowed to do what they asked, and sends back a response.

In a traditional data center that server is a physical machine you own. In AWS
the server is usually virtual. You provision it when you need it and remove it
when you are done, and the bill follows that lifecycle. Two services show up
constantly as examples: Amazon EC2, which gives you virtual servers you launch
and terminate, and Amazon S3, which stores and retrieves objects such as files
and backups.

## Why "pay for what you use" matters

When you run your own data center you pay for peak capacity all year, even though
you only need that peak for a few hours or a few weeks. You also pay whether the
machines are busy or idle. The cloud breaks that link. Provision resources for
the work in front of you, and when the work stops, stop the resources and the
charges stop with them, subject to the specifics of each service.

This is the shift from capital expense to operating expense. Buying servers is a
large upfront capital cost. Paying for cloud usage is a variable operating cost
that tracks demand. That single change is the foundation for most of the cloud
benefits you will study next.

<Callout type="note" title="A useful mental picture">
Think of ordering coffee. Your order is the request, the barista is the server,
paying and showing ID is authentication and authorization, and the coffee is the
response. You pay per cup, not for the whole cafe.
</Callout>

## Deployment models

AWS groups deployments into three models, and a question will often describe a
situation and expect you to name it. A cloud deployment is fully deployed in the
cloud, with every part of the application running on cloud services, whether it
was built there or migrated there. A hybrid deployment connects cloud resources
to existing resources that are not in the cloud, most commonly an on-premises
data center, which is what a company mid-migration or one keeping a regulated
system in house ends up with. An on-premises deployment, which AWS sometimes
calls the private cloud, means deploying resources in your own data center using
virtualization and resource management tools. It can provide dedicated
resources, but it does not provide many of the benefits of cloud computing.

## What to take away

You should be able to state the definition from memory, name the deployment
model a scenario is describing, and explain why usage-based billing lets a
business stop paying for idle capacity. The definition and the economics are the
spine of Cloud Concepts; the client-server picture is the mental model the rest
of the course rests on.
