AWS Support is sold in tiers, and the exam tests which tier a scenario needs.
The differences come down to who can open technical cases, how fast AWS responds,
how much of Trusted Advisor you can see, and whether you get a named contact at
AWS. This lesson covers the tiers, then Trusted Advisor.

<Callout type="note" title="Plans are changing, so verify before exam day">
AWS has announced that it is consolidating its support plans. The current
documentation lists end-of-support dates for Developer, Business, and Enterprise
On-Ramp, with Developer and Business moving to a plan called Business Support
plus, and Enterprise On-Ramp customers moving to Enterprise Support, all by
January 1, 2027. The CLF-C02 exam guide and most current questions still describe
the five established tiers below, so learn them, but check the live AWS Support
plans page near your exam date in case the tested model has shifted.
</Callout>

## Basic Support

Basic Support is included for every AWS account at no cost. It covers account and
billing questions, service quota increase requests, access to documentation and
the support forums, and service health checks. It does not include technical
support cases for how-to or troubleshooting. From Trusted Advisor, Basic gets the
core set of checks, not the full set.

## Developer Support

Developer Support adds technical support over email through Support Center, during
business hours. Cases can be opened by one contact, and the response targets are
general guidance within 24 hours and a system-impaired case within 12 hours. It
suits early development and testing, where one person needs to ask AWS technical
questions but no production workload is at stake.

## Business Support

Business Support is the first tier built for production workloads. It allows
unlimited cases opened by any user with the right permissions, adds 24x7 access
to Cloud Support Engineers by phone, chat, and email, and gives the full set of
Trusted Advisor checks rather than the core subset. Its response targets add a
production-system-down commitment of less than one hour and a production-impaired
commitment of less than four hours, on top of the lower-severity targets.

<Callout type="key" title="Basic and Developer versus Business and up">
The line that matters most: Business Support is the first tier with 24x7
technical support, unlimited cases for any authorized user, and the full set of
Trusted Advisor checks. Basic and Developer do not have a production-down
response commitment. A scenario about a production workload needing fast help
points to Business or higher.
</Callout>

## Enterprise On-Ramp

Enterprise On-Ramp sits between Business and Enterprise. It adds a faster
response target of less than 30 minutes for business-critical cases, access to a
pool of Technical Account Managers rather than a single designated one, and
consultative reviews. It suits organizations that need quicker critical response
and some strategic guidance but do not yet need a full Enterprise relationship.

## Enterprise Support

Enterprise Support is the top tier. It adds a designated Technical Account Manager
who knows your environment, the fastest response target of less than 15 minutes
for business-critical or mission-critical cases, and the broadest set of
proactive guidance and event support. Reach for Enterprise when you run critical
workloads at scale and want a named AWS contact and the fastest help.

<Callout type="tip" title="Who gets a Technical Account Manager">
A designated Technical Account Manager comes with Enterprise Support. Enterprise
On-Ramp gives access to a TAM pool rather than a single designated one. Basic,
Developer, and Business do not include a TAM. If a question hinges on a named,
dedicated AWS contact, the answer is Enterprise.
</Callout>

## AWS Trusted Advisor

AWS Trusted Advisor inspects your account against AWS best practices and
recommends changes. Its checks are grouped into categories: cost optimization,
performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits, and operational
excellence. Cost optimization flags idle or underused resources, security flags
risky configurations such as open ports or missing MFA on the root user, and
service limits warns when you are approaching a quota.

How much you see depends on your support plan. Basic and Developer get a core set
of checks, while Business, Enterprise On-Ramp, and Enterprise unlock the full set
across every category. So Trusted Advisor coverage is itself one of the reasons
to move up a tier.

## What to take away

The five established tiers are Basic, Developer, Business, Enterprise On-Ramp, and
Enterprise. Basic is free and covers account and billing only. Developer adds
business-hours technical support for one contact. Business is the first tier with
24x7 support, unlimited cases, a production-down response target, and the full
Trusted Advisor checks. Enterprise On-Ramp adds faster critical response and a TAM
pool. Enterprise adds a designated Technical Account Manager and the fastest
response. Trusted Advisor checks span cost, performance, security, fault
tolerance, service limits, and operational excellence, with full coverage on
Business and above. Remember that AWS has announced changes to this lineup, so
verify near exam day.
