AWS Support is sold in tiers, and the exam tests which tier a scenario needs.
The differences come down to who can open technical cases, how fast AWS responds,
how much of Trusted Advisor you can see, and whether you get a named contact at
AWS. This lesson covers the tiers, then Trusted Advisor, then the technical
resources AWS publishes outside the plans.

<Callout type="note" title="Plans are changing, so verify before exam day">
AWS is consolidating its support plans. The documentation now lists four: Basic,
AWS Business Support+, AWS Enterprise Support, and AWS Unified Operations, with
Unified Operations sitting past Enterprise Support. Developer, Business, and
Enterprise On-Ramp are all discontinued on January 1, 2027. Developer and
Business customers can keep their plan until then or move to Business Support+
whenever they choose, Enterprise On-Ramp customers are upgraded to Enterprise
Support automatically during 2026, and all three plans stay available in the AWS
GovCloud (US) Region. The CLF-C02 exam guide still names the five established
tiers below, so learn those for the exam, but check the live AWS Support plans
page near your exam date in case the tested model has shifted.
</Callout>

## Basic Support

Basic Support is included for every AWS account at no cost. It covers account and
billing questions, service quota increase requests, access to documentation and
the support forums, and service health checks. It does not include technical
support cases for how-to or troubleshooting. From Trusted Advisor, Basic gets the
core set of checks, not the full set.

## Developer Support

Developer Support adds technical support over email through Support Center, during
business hours. Cases can be opened by one contact, and the response targets are
general guidance within 24 business hours and a system-impaired case within 12
business hours. Business hours means roughly 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM in the country
set on the account, excluding weekends and holidays, so a 24-business-hour clock
can run across several calendar days. It suits early development and testing,
where one person needs to ask AWS technical questions but no production workload
is at stake.

## Business Support

Business Support is the first tier built for production workloads. It allows
unlimited cases opened by any user with the right permissions, adds 24x7 access
to Cloud Support Engineers by phone, chat, and web, and gives the full set of
Trusted Advisor checks rather than the core subset. Its response targets add a
production-system-down commitment of less than one hour and a production-impaired
commitment of less than four hours, on top of the lower-severity targets.

<Callout type="key" title="Basic and Developer versus Business and up">
The line that matters most: Business Support is the first tier with 24x7
technical support, unlimited cases for any authorized user, and the full set of
Trusted Advisor checks. Basic and Developer do not have a production-down
response commitment. A scenario about a production workload needing fast help
points to Business or higher.
</Callout>

## Enterprise On-Ramp

Enterprise On-Ramp sits between Business and Enterprise. It adds a faster
response target of less than 30 minutes for business-critical cases, access to
Technical Account Managers, and consultative engagements such as business
reviews, guided Well-Architected reviews, and cost optimization workshops. The
distinction the exam draws is that the TAM access here is not a designated TAM.
It suits organizations that need quicker critical response and some strategic
guidance but do not yet need a full Enterprise relationship.

## Enterprise Support

Enterprise Support is the top of the five tiers the exam names. It adds a
designated Technical Account Manager who knows your environment, a response
target of less than 15 minutes for business-critical or mission-critical cases,
and the broadest proactive guidance and event support of those five tiers. Reach
for Enterprise when you run critical workloads at scale and want a named AWS
contact and the fastest response the exam-era lineup offers.

<Callout type="tip" title="Who gets a Technical Account Manager">
A designated Technical Account Manager comes with Enterprise Support. Enterprise
On-Ramp gives access to Technical Account Managers, but not a designated one.
Basic, Developer, and Business do not include a TAM. If a question hinges on a
named, dedicated AWS contact, the answer is Enterprise.
</Callout>

## AWS Trusted Advisor

AWS Trusted Advisor inspects your account against AWS best practices and
recommends changes. Its checks are grouped into categories: cost optimization,
performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits, and operational
excellence. Cost optimization flags idle or underused resources, security flags
risky configurations such as open ports or missing MFA on the root user, and
service limits warns when you are approaching a quota.

How much you see depends on your support plan. Basic and Developer get a core set
of checks, while Business, Enterprise On-Ramp, and Enterprise unlock the full set
across every category. So Trusted Advisor coverage is itself one of the reasons
to move up a tier. The rule survives the plan consolidation: in AWS's current
lineup the plans with the full check set are Business Support+, Enterprise
Support, and Unified Operations, and Basic still gets only a subset.

## AWS technical resources

The exam also asks about the resources AWS publishes outside the support plans.

AWS re:Post is AWS's community question-and-answer service, part of the AWS Free
Tier. Driven by AWS customers, Partners, and employees, it gives crowd-sourced,
expert-reviewed answers to technical questions, and it replaced the original AWS
Forums. It also connects to AWS Support: a question from a paid support plan that
the community does not answer goes to AWS Support engineers, who open a case if
the problem is specific to that customer's workload.

AWS Marketplace is a curated digital catalog for finding, buying, deploying, and
managing third-party software, data, and services. Listings come in formats such
as AMIs and SaaS, and AWS handles the billing, so the charges appear on your AWS
bill.

The AWS Partner Network is the global community of organizations that build
solutions and services on AWS. It spans software partners, whose products run on
or work with AWS services, and services partners such as consultancies, systems
integrators, resellers, and managed service providers.

AWS Whitepapers and Guides is AWS's library of technical whitepapers, decision
guides, technical guides, reference material, and reference architecture
diagrams, authored by AWS and the AWS community. Documentation and best practice
guides come with Basic Support, so every account can read them at no cost.

## What to take away

The five tiers the exam guide names are Basic, Developer, Business, Enterprise
On-Ramp, and Enterprise. Basic is free and covers account and billing only.
Developer adds business-hours technical support for one contact. Business is the
first tier with 24x7 support, unlimited cases, a production-down response target,
and the full Trusted Advisor checks. Enterprise On-Ramp adds faster critical
response and TAM access without a designated TAM. Enterprise adds a designated
Technical Account Manager and a 15-minute business-critical response target.
Trusted Advisor checks span cost, performance, security, fault tolerance, service
limits, and operational excellence, with full coverage on Business and above.
Outside the plans, re:Post answers technical questions, Marketplace sells
third-party software on your AWS bill, the Partner Network is the community of
partner organizations, and Whitepapers and Guides is AWS's technical library.
Remember that AWS has changed this lineup since the exam guide was written, so
verify near exam day.
